Picture your own business

Same kind of problem. Different costume.

The system changes shape for the business. The promise stays simple: respond faster, lose fewer opportunities, and give the owner a clearer day.

Real estate agent

The problem

A lead arrives from an ad or website. Their phone number is in a spreadsheet, an inbox, or three different apps. Nobody knows who called, what they wanted, or what happens next.

The build

Capture the lead, tag the source, place the opportunity in a pipeline, alert the agent, send a permission-based first reply, and create the next follow-up task. For a seller, add the right questions before the calendar link.

Plumber

The problem

The phone rings during a repair. The caller has water coming through the ceiling. They do not want a brochure. They want to know whether somebody can help.

The build

Give the caller a clear first response, collect address and urgency, separate emergencies from routine jobs, notify the on-call human, and follow up with people who asked for an estimate.

Electrician

The problem

Every request sounds urgent. Some are panel upgrades. Some are lights. Some need a licensed human immediately. The inbox does not sort any of it.

The build

Use a short intake that asks the useful questions, routes the request, books qualified estimates, and keeps the record attached to the customer instead of trapped in a text thread.

Hairstylist or salon

The problem

Appointments live in DMs. Cancellations arrive after hours. A new client asks the same five questions that were answered yesterday.

The build

Create a friendly booking path, answer common questions, collect preferences, send reminders, and invite the client back at the right time without making the business feel robotic.

Dentist office

The problem

The front desk is balancing calls, insurance questions, confirmations, cancellations, and patients standing in front of them.

The build

Build a careful intake and scheduling assistant, confirmation and recall workflows, staff alerts, and clear handoffs. Dental systems need a conservative privacy posture and a final compliance review by the practice.

Any local business

The problem

You are good at the work. You are not always available at the exact moment a stranger decides to call, click, or buy.

The build

Map the customer journey, remove the dead ends, automate the repeatable pieces, and keep the owner in control of the moments that require experience and judgment.

Artificial intelligence for real people

You do not need to “talk like a robot.” You just need to explain what you want.

Think of AI as a very fast assistant who can read, write, sort, compare, and help you think. It does not know your business until you teach it. It should not make the final call on sensitive or expensive decisions.

Tell it the job

“I run a plumbing company. Write a short reply to a homeowner who has asked for an estimate.”

Plain words work.

Give it the rules

“Keep it warm. Do not promise a time until my office confirms it. Ask for the address and best callback number.”

Rules create consistency.

Check the result

Read it like you would read an employee’s draft. Fix anything that sounds wrong. Then save the good version for the next time.

You stay in charge.

Three prompts you can use today

“Turn these rough notes into a clear customer email. Keep every fact. Ask me when something is missing.”
“Look at these 20 leads and group them by urgency, service needed, and next action. Do not guess missing facts.”
“Explain this technology like I am smart, busy, and new to it. Use one example from my business.”
